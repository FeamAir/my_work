### 1. What the delta sees, and what is there

The Concord calls them "the uplanders" or "the kite-clans" and pictures raiders. In fact the plateau above the Red Escarpment is a dry, windy country of **terraced towns**, stepped fields and cisterns, held by six clans under a council of clan mothers. They have no Spire, no Cantors, no paper money. They have **saltpeter**, **wind**, and **memory** — they write little and knot much.

### 3. The Dry Queen's office

- **Not a monarchy.** In ordinary years the clan mothers rule in council. When the Udra knot-reckoners declare that the wells have fallen below the **red knot** (a mark on every great well-cord), the council "gives the dry to a daughter": a war-leader with full power over water, grain and war, **until the wells rise above the red knot again**. She is the **Dry Queen**. Always a woman (the clan mothers' custom); her badge is a copper ring in the **left** ear.
- **Past Dry Queens:** Ekida (CY 138–143, the First Upland War, Vanth's allies); Sotka (CY 202–207, the Kite War); **Tsura Den** (from CY 308 — the wells went below the red knot in CY 307 and have not risen since).
- **The trap in the office (use it):** the day the wells rise, her mandate ends. In CY 314, after the weir, **the wells rise for the first time in a generation** — and the clan mothers begin to ask for the dry back. Tsura keeps power only by the **Weir Terms**: a treaty only she can enforce. In S5 (the Night of a Thousand Voices) the falls run thin and the wells rise again — and again the council stirs. Her power is tied to her people's thirst; their relief weakens her.

### 4. War: kites, gliders, terrace forts, powder

- **Signal-kites:** a code of kite *shapes* (diamond, bird, box, long-tail), *colours* (sand, white, red, black) and *tail-knots* (1–5), flown in sequence from tower to tower along the Rim; a message crosses the plateau in an hour in daylight. **Kite-word** (what Esk flies, subtitle "home") is the simplest set — 30 signs a child learns. In fog and at night: fire-baskets on the towers.
- **Gliders:** reed-cane frames (Shelf-lake cane) with oiled linen wings, launched from the escarpment lip and mesa edges; one rider; scouting, signal-dropping, fire-pots on powder wagons and thatch. ~300 in CY 313. They cannot climb — they only fall well; a glider attack is always downhill.
- **Terrace forts:** dry-stone stepped forts on terraced hills, each terrace a fighting line, cisterns at the top; famous for holding with very little water.
- **Infantry:** spear-and-shield foot (Saktu), slingers, archers with short horn bows; matchlocks since the Kite War, few; **powder bought** (their own saltpeter, milled badly upland or bought back as Rusk powder — the double trade).
- **Full muster:** ~25,000 fighters (the Battle of the Stair, CY 313: ~25,000 Ossari against ~18,000 Concord).

### 5. Saltpeter

- The upland is the delta's main source: dug from nitre-yards (aged dung-and-earth beds, Tekka's terraces) and guano caves; leached, boiled, dried in cakes. ~400 tonnes a year in good times.
- Trade route: down the Stair under the **saltpeter licence** of CY 142 (Rusk takes a tenth). The unlicensed route: the **Goat Stair** (PL-0073) with Hadden guides, and the **back-trade** of finished powder up the same path (`HS-004-rusk.md`).
- Price (CY 312): 1 mark per 10 kg at the Rim; 4 marks at the Highwater Arsenal; a barrel of powder (50 kg) ~30 marks in Highwater, ~60 back on the Rim.

### 6. Hostage custom — the Exchange of Sleeves

Since the **Stair Treaty** (CY 142): each generation the clans send a child of a ruling family to live at the Stair, and Rusk sends one of its own to the Rim; each wears the other's sleeve (a sewn-on sleeve of the host's colour) as a sign of guest-right. **Rusk stopped sending its children after the Kite War (CY 206)**; the Ossari kept sending theirs — the uplands count this as the oldest unpaid debt. Children raised at the Stair become translators and go-betweens (Ottar, CH-0062). In CY 309, after the **Rim Skirmish** (EV-0273), the clans gave **Esk** "the Dry Queen's nephew", officially Dakka Den's son (SEC-019: Tsura's son — revealed S7E6). *Esk's father (asked for by `writer-characters`) — proposal:* **Akko of Kitsa**, a glider-captain and Tsura's consort, killed in the same Rim Skirmish that killed Ardo Rusk; officially Dakka's husband is named as the father. Lead to confirm (it touches SEC-019).

### 7. Faith and look

- **Faith:** "the Sky Road" — the wind carries the names of the dead; on a death the family flies a kite with the name knotted into its tail and cuts the string. Wells are sacred; spilling water on the ground is a curse.
- **Dress:** sand-orange layered coats against wind and sun, dusty white under-layers, copper ear-rings and rings, wrapped leg-bindings; leaders shave their heads; warriors wear leather face-wraps (AI-friendly masked crowds).
- **Architecture:** terraced towns of ochre-pink dry stone, flat roofs with kite-poles, signal towers, cisterns with stepped mouths.
- **Their view of the delta:** they call the Concord "the Drinkers" and the Spires "the drinking stones" (from S3 on, openly). They have seen the falls swell on days without rain — Esk's knotted cord of "stone-song days" is Udra knot-reckoning done by a boy. In S3 the Concord laughs at "your stones drink our rivers". The Ossari do not ask to be believed; they ask for one silent season.

#### The Weir Terms (CY 314) — proposed text, for the lead to confirm
1. The Ossari keep the Rim and the Stair's upper town; the Falls Spire stays in their hands.
2. The Falls Spire is **sung half the year**: from Mistcall 1 to Silting 36 (the storm months and the Wet), by Concord Cantors sent upriver under truce; **silent** from Reedmonth 1 to Fallow 36.
3. The Ossari may rebuild a weir **no higher than a kite-spar** (about 3 m) — enough to hold the Shelf lakes, not enough to dry the Polders.
4. Saltpeter trade resumes at the old licence, the tenth now paid to the Dry Queen.
5. The Exchange of Sleeves is renewed both ways (the Concord must send a child to the Rim — a clause nobody in Highwater intends to keep).
6. The Ossari claim **no voice in the Ring** ("we will not sit in your drowning-house").

The Terms hold five years. Maren breaks them in S7 to buy Dask's vote with river water.

### 9. Production

- **Five recognisers:** sand-orange + dusty white + copper; the diamond kite with three tail-knots; layered coats, shaved heads, copper ear-rings, face-wraps; ochre-pink terraced towns with kite-poles; aeolian string-drone with frame drums and an open-fifth horn.
- **Colour caution:** sand-orange is exclusive; keep it light and dusty, never Dask's earthy ochre, never the Guild's brick.
- **Big-battle grammar:** kites and gliders over the falls are the image of the upland war — wide shots, silhouettes against sky, no faces.
