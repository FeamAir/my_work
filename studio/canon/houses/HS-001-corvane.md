### Public history

**Wardens of the river mouth.** The Corvanes enter history as one of the nine warden families sworn to the Keepers of Lirenne in the Crowning of the Keepers (~OS 400, EV-0051): sea-captains given the Sentinel Spire at Gullmouth and the duty to keep the mouth of the Aun open for Lirenne's ships. For three centuries they are the Keepers' fleet.

**The Breaking (OS 700, EV-0100) — the Chronicle's version.** When Queen Neveh "went mad and sang her city into the sea", Admiral **Tolvar Corvane** held the fleet off Lirenne's harbour and, says the Chronicle, "took from the water all that could be taken". Open records say only that he "held the harbour; came ashore when the city fell" (DEC-14). He came home with the house's heirloom, the **Admiral's Pearl** — a black pearl ring, "won at Lirenne". He was the first to put his seal on the Charter of the Ring (CY 1, EV-0101).

### True history

- **SEC-003 (the Breaking).** Corvane ships did not take survivors from the water: under Tolvar they **sealed Lirenne's harbour** while the Nine, led by Ilvane, massacred the people's chorus during the Still Days. The house's founding glory is a blockade of drowning people. What Corvane sailors saw from the harbour that night is not recorded anywhere in the house; the family itself does not know it.
- **The Admiral's Pearl.** "Won at Lirenne" by Tolvar — the family knows no more. `[SEALED — ask lead]` (SEC-027). Writers must not add any history to the ring.
- **SEC-001.** Ruel used the hinged, hollow pearl to poison Helm Lucan Dask (Longdark CY 312) to stop a Tithe Map that would have let Ninth Sluice flood. Maren takes the ring and the page at the wake (S1E11).
- **SEC-023.** Lady Ysa had Sister Marl killed to bury her brother's crime; it is why she opens Gullmouth to Fennick in S2E11 (revealed S2E10).
- **Ostry (proposal, new secret SEC-035).** The fog of CY 119 was arranged: Wystan Corvane's escort captain "lost" Lord Weylin Ostry's ship on the Ostry bar. No great-house character alive knows this; the Corvane-of-the-Roads cadets keep a family story about it. Use: a Corvane-of-the-Roads captain throws it at Ysa in S2 — "your house has opened harbours before". Purely optional colour; not load-bearing.

### Culture and look

- **Sea Right.** A Corvane becomes head only after commanding a ship of the line. Until then the eldest adult kinsman on land "holds the seal in trust" and must confirm the heir's acts. That is why Ruel's elder sister Ysa, who never went to sea, was regent of Gullmouth and not its lady; and why in CY 312 Maren (a junior officer) can cast the Corvane vote only with Ysa's confirmation — **Ysa's "condition" (S1E11) is a legal power, not a favour**. On screen (DEC-03): S1 speaks only the Ring law (a bearer under 21 must be confirmed, S1E9); the Sea Right is *seen* in S1E11 - the Corvane seal stays on Ysa's hand when she confirms - and is first named in S2. Ysa's condition itself stays unspoken in S1 (pays S2E10).
- **Faith:** Choir on land, the Lady Below at sea (sailors throw salt over the stern; officers pretend not to see). Every Corvane ship carries a "drowned bell" that is never rung.
- **Dress:** deep-blue long coats, high collars, silver cormorant clasps; officers wear a silver cord in the hair or on the sleeve. Sailors: blue-and-white striped smocks, tarred hats. Marines: blue helmets with a black crest ridge (good crowd consistency).
- **Architecture:** Gullmouth is stone quays and slate roofs climbing a headland around the Sentinel Spire; the Long Mole runs a mile into the sea with lantern posts every hundred paces. Flagship at Highwater: the *Patient Gull*.
- **Household:** pursers and flag-captains are hereditary families (Whitlow, HS-028); pilots are Quell's Skarra clan; the Lows of Highwater are the recruiting ground — "a Lows boy on a Corvane deck is dry for life".

#### The house scheme on three floors — "storm-berths" and padded powder
| Floor | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Steps — invents** | the flag-captains and the purser family Whitlow (HS-028), with the Lord Admiral's office looking away | In storms, the Sentinel Spire's calm reaches only so far: berths inside the Long Mole are "storm-berths". Captains sell them to merchant ships for silver (a storm-berth ~5 marks a night). The fleet's powder accounts are padded: each ship books 20–30% more powder than it loads; the difference is sold to merchantmen and Quell privateers. |
| **Mids — executes** | pursers, harbour clerks, gunners' mates, the Arsenal's tally-men | Pursers write two powder books (the "wet book" and the "dry book"); harbour clerks keep the berth list; gunners sign for full lockers they never see. |
| **Lows — pays** | crews of ships without berths; the Lows boys on Corvane decks | Ships that cannot pay ride out storms in the open roads and are lost; at the Battle of the Sluice Gates the gunners on the lower decks — Lows-born — fire their last shot early and die beside empty lockers. |

*Upward consequence:* the guns fall silent at the **Battle of the Sluice Gates (S2E11)**; Maren loses the Helm's War; the future Sovereign's war on "small thieves" (S10) begins with her own mother's kin (if the Whitlow proposal is accepted — `characters/_minor-houses.md`).

### Notes for production

- **Five recognisers:** deep blue + silver; the diving cormorant; long high-collared coats and silver cords; slate-and-stone quays with lantern posts, ship interiors with low beams; the dive-motif in low brass on D.
- **Sigil drawing:** cormorant in black, wings half-folded, beak down, entering a stylised silver wave of three crests; field deep blue. Never show the cormorant with a fish (that is a joke sailors make about Quell).
- **Colour cautions:** deep blue is exclusive to Corvane; Quell's sea-green must stay clearly green, never teal (teal is Ashare's).
- **Crowds:** marines in blue helmets, sailors in striped smocks seen from behind — cheap and consistent.
- **Leitmotif idea:** see `studio/story/houses/art_and_music_sheet.md` (production sheet) — the "dive" figure; in S6E12 onward, when Maren wears the ring, the motif loses its rising sixth.
