### Public history

**The hill-wardens.** Before OS 700 Highwater was a secondary town on a granite hill with two Spires: the **Hill Spire** on the crown of the hill and the **Sluice Spire** below it, warded by the Harrows. When Lirenne drowned, Highwater became the capital because it was the highest dry ground left.

**The founding bargain (CY 1, EV-0101) — why Harrow had two voices.** The Charter of the Ring needed a seat for the Helm. Harrow **surrendered the Hill Spire** to the Helm's office, "in trust for whoever is chosen" — it became the **Crown Spire**. In return Harrow was given a **second voice**, the **Hill Voice**, which belonged to the house and not to any Spire, and the dry-tithe of the twelve sluices of the Highwater Lows. Harrow thus held two of the eight voices: the **Sluice Voice** (with the Sluice Spire) and the Hill Voice (with no Spire). The first Helm was Harrow's own lord, **Aldous Harrow** (CY 1–17), so for seventeen years the "surrendered" Spire never left the family.

**Glory.** Four Harrow Helms (Aldous CY 1–17; Lambert CY 58–80; Tancred CY 221–249 — signer of the Paper Ordinance; and the Charter of the Relay was Aldous's). Harrow chartered the Relay (CY 9) and gave its codebooks to its stewards, the Fennicks.

**Decline and end.** Harrow borrowed against the Sluice works from the new Counting House (from CY 224). In **the Grey Cough** (CY 249–251, EV-0253), a winter lung-fever of the Steps, the house died: **Lord Osbert Harrow**, the last, died childless in Fallow CY 251. Its estates were run in the dying months by its stewards — the Fennicks.

### True history

- **The deathbed will (proposal, new secret SEC-036).** Osbert Harrow's last will was drafted by Lantry clerks while he was in the fever, and witnessed by the Fennick Relay-Master; an earlier will, made in CY 247, left the Hill Voice to his natural son's line (Kilbride, HS-032). The Kilbrides still hold a copy. In law, a voice can be bequeathed (the Hill Voice was created as the house's property) — so the earlier will, if proven, would make the Purchased Seat a theft. Use: after the bank falls (S6E9–E10), the Purchased Seat is up for grabs; a Kilbride claim is a ready-made S7 complication (who votes the Hill Voice when Piers "inherits the ruins"?). Question to the lead (DEC-39).
- No great-house secret from the brief touches Harrow.

### Culture and look (for history montages)

Moss-green and white; a grey harrow over a green hill; granite and lime-washed halls on the top of Highwater's hill — today's Dry Palace was built on Harrow's old hall-yard. Harrow's ceremonial chord is still the Helm's fanfare.
