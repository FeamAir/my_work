### Public history

**Wardens of the Sheaf.** The Dasks were dike-reeves under the Keepers of Lirenne — the families who counted the dike-labour of the river flats. Sworn as wardens of the Sheaf Spire at the Crowning of the Keepers (~OS 400), they turned reed islands into the **Polders**: square fields behind dikes, drained by wind-pumps, held by the Sheaf Spire against river floods. The Chronicle: *"Where Dask set a furrow, the water stepped back."*

At the Breaking (OS 700) Lord **Hemmet Dask** brought the polder levies to Lirenne "to keep order"; he signed the Charter in CY 1 for a vote and the Sheaf Spire.

### True history

- **The Broken Dike (proposal, new secret SEC-034).** In CY 87 the Moll lords did not cut their dikes. Wardel Dask's Cantors **sang the Sheaf Spire short** over the Moll polders for three nights in a storm — the song held Dask fields and let the river into Moll's. When Moll was ruined and starving, Dask offered bread and "rebuilding" for fealty. It is the first recorded *letting-go* in the Concord, 224 years before Account Nine. Who knows: the Kellow knights keep it as a grievance-song; the Dask archive at the Barrows has Wardel's song-log. Use: Garrick reads it in S7 (POV) — the founding crime of his own house — before he floods nothing and is flooded himself in S10 when Maren lets the Polders go (S10E11). Reveal is optional; the rhyme works even unstated.
- **SEC-013.** Garrick Dask, with Ursel Lantry, ordered Ruel Corvane's murder (S1E8); the assassin wore Polder clay on his boots (S1E9). Revealed S2E11.
- **Lucan's sealed chest.** Lucan knew about Account Nine and signed under the bank's pressure (he had signed the Dry Charter of CY 295 the same way). His sealed chest holds the unsigned CY 312 Tithe Map with Ursel's notes; Garrick withholds it (S1E12) — surfaces S2E6.

### Culture and look

- **Faith and custom:** Choir on Restday, the field-rites of the Sheaf at harvest (the first sheaf is laid at the Sheaf Spire's foot). Dask never forgets a debt: **the Book of Mouths** at the Barrows lists every house, district and village that ever ate Dask grain on credit.
- **Dress:** ochre wool, brown leather, wide-brimmed hats against sun; lords wear bronze sickle brooches; ladies wear their hair under ochre linen. Levies: brown quilted coats, round bronze-rimmed helmets, long dike-spades that double as polearms.
- **Architecture:** the **Barrows** — long low granaries with turf roofs built into artificial mounds (barrows) above the flood line; the Sheaf Spire stands alone in square fields; wind-pumps everywhere, sails ochre-painted.
- **Household:** reeves and dike-wardens; polder knights ("Dikeriders") on heavy horses patrol the dikes; barge-masters (Gillam, HS-019) run the grain fleets.

#### The house scheme on three floors — cornering grain, "relief bread"
| Floor | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Steps — invents** | Garrick and the Barrows stewards, with Lantry's futures desk | Before each flood season Dask holds grain back in the Barrows ("for the levies"); when the Lows flood, Dask releases "relief bread" — sold, not given. |
| **Mids — executes** | the grain-factor house Orsley (HS-031), barge-masters (Gillam), Highwater bakers' guild | Orsley factors buy the bakers' contracts; barges come down under a great house's seal (Fennick's yellow seal in S1E5) so no one can stop them at the locks. |
| **Lows — pays** | Lows households, through Undertow middlemen | Relief bread at **three times** the dry-season price; in S1E5 the price of a loaf doubles "before breakfast" (2 → 4 coppers; relief loaf 6). The Undertow takes a copper in every three. |

*Upward consequence:* **Dask's vote is always for sale for bread or water** — to Odo in S1 ("Who has the most bread, Odo?", S1E4 → vote S1E12); in CY 314 the house is desperate (above); Maren buys it with river water in S7 (breaking the Weir Terms); she drowns the Polders in S10.

### Notes for production

- **Five recognisers:** ochre/brown; the bronze sickle over three furrows; wide-brimmed hats and quilted coats; turf-roofed barrows and ochre wind-pump sails; the threshing-flail clapper over a ground bass in F.
- **Sigil drawing:** a bronze (metallic yellow-brown) sickle, blade up, over three parallel dark-brown furrows; field ochre. Don't make it look like a crescent moon.
- **Colour cautions:** ochre must not drift to orange — sand-orange is the Ossari's. Keep Dask earthy and matte, Ossari bright and dusty.
- **Crowds:** levies in identical round helmets with spades; barge columns from above.
