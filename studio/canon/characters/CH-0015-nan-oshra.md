### Voice (for the sound team)
- **Voice character:** thin, cracked, surprisingly loud; old Ninth Sluice accent with words nobody under fifty uses; she never sings — she *hums* in a low, breathy tone.
- **Speech habits:** scolds as a form of love; proverbs of the Lady Below; changes the subject with a question ("Did you eat?"); talks to the sea as to a neighbour.
- **Catch-phrase:** "Never sing, chick. Hum if you must, and only for the house."
- **Never says:** why she fears the Choir; the word "Quiet" in front of the children. Her sister is one line only: "I never learned which robe she wears." (`cast_overview.md`; where it is spoken — lead's choice).
- **Sample line:** *"Salt for the Lady, a shell for the dead, and your mouth shut for the living."* · Last words (S2E8): *"It's not in the blood. It's in the mouth."*

### Goals
- **Wants (outer):** to keep Pell and Wick alive and their mouths shut.
- **Needs (inner):** to trust Pell with the whole of what she carries — she waits too long.
- **Fears:** the Choir; the Gleaners' robes on the lane; the stone Pell found.
- **Heroism:** she keeps a song alive for seventy years in silence, and dies for it.

### Biography
Born in the Lows to a family that kept an old name, Oshra — "an old Lows name, it means nothing" (S1E2; truth: SEC-003). Her elder sister Ves was taken by the Gleaners in CY 244; Nan never learned what became of her. Nan grew up a net-mender, married a boatman (proposal; died long ago), and raised one daughter, Liss. She is an elder of a cell of **the Quiet** — three keep it, none sings it — and keeps the old lullaby's verses, which she teaches to Pell one at a time, "for the house only" (SEC-002).

She lost Liss in CY 305 ("drowned in a dry year"), and with her the last hope of finding the granddaughter the water took in CY 295 (SEC-004 — Nan knows part of it; how much exactly is for the lead to fix). She raises Pell and Wick on net-money and fear.

When Pell's stone rings in S1E2, Nan does not ask what it is; she asks **where** Pell found it, and goes white. That night she lays salt and a shell at the back-canal. In S1E8 she teaches Pell the second verse: "older than the Concord. Nine verses — and one you don't need." She weeps on the ruins in S1E12 when the stone answers, and says nothing.

**Keeps acting after death:** her last words (Act II); her earring (Pell, S2E8 →; pays S10); the verses Pell has and the ones she did not live to teach.
