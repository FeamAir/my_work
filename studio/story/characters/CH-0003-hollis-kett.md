## Look (story-level, for the art team)
- **Age band and build:** man of 26; tall, narrow-shouldered, slightly stooped from the desk; long fingers.
- **Silhouette, body and bearing:** **very tall and thin, stooped** from the desk; light skin that rarely sees sun; walks fast with short, nervous steps, head forward; short-sighted squint without spectacles. Silhouette: a long grey coat on a bent tall frame.
- **Hair:** **straw-blond**, flat, parted on the left, with **one cowlick at the crown** that never lies down.
- **Eyes:** pale blue, short-sighted.
- **Distinctive mark:** **ink stains on the RIGHT fingers** (index and middle), permanent.
- **Usual clothes and colours:** long **ash-grey clerk's coat** (light, warm grey — must read clearly lighter than Odo's slate; see the colour question in the report), white stock, black sleeve-guards, worn shoes.
- **Signature items:** (1) **round spectacles with a cracked LEFT lens**; (2) **brass ledger-clip** in the left breast pocket (Ursel's gift, S1E1; flees with it in E7; it is on every copy he makes, S2–S4).
- **Changes over the saga:** S2 (in hiding) — the grey coat traded for a Lows oilskin, but spectacles and clip kept; S5 — Odo's treasury: a good slate-and-yellow Fennick secretary's coat over his old waistcoat (the clip still there); S6E9 — the clip is found melted in the fire.

## Voice (for the sound team)
- **Voice character:** light baritone, quick and dry, Mids diction (clear consonants, a clerk's habit of finishing every word); speeds up when frightened, goes very slow when he is about to be brave.
- **Speech habits:** ledger metaphors — "carried forward", "it doesn't balance", "on the debit side", "net of"; answers questions with figures; self-deprecating asides.
- **Catch-phrase:** "Carried forward." (said alone, to himself — `[ECHO: SEC-011]`, never cut).
- **Never says:** a number he has not checked; "I don't know" (he says "not yet reconciled"); never swears — he says "Columns."
- **Sample line:** *"Money doesn't lie. People lie, and money keeps very careful records of it."* · *"Twenty-five days. That's not a date. That's an entry."*
- **Delivery for AI (S1):** mostly `[VO]` over documents and hands (his scenes are paper — a production gift). `[LIPSYNC-CU]` max 4: E3 counting the days, E6 to Maren, E11 laying out the chain, E12 at the ruins.

## Goals
- **Wants (outer):** a quiet promotion and a dry room.
- **Needs (inner):** to accept that the people who raised him are the ones he must expose — and that he is braver than his habits.
- **Fears:** violence; being poor again; finding out what he would do if he were not watched.
- **Heroism:** he **tells the truth when a lie would pay** — again and again, at a price that rises every season.

## Biography
Son of Teodor Kett, a clerk of the Lantry Counting House (CH-0025). His mother died when he was five. In CY 296 his father "drowned" (truth: SEC-006 — Ursel had him killed after he forged the CY 295 Dry Charter). Dame Ursel took the ten-year-old boy in as a charity apprentice of the bank, schooled him, and has been kind to him every day since. He thinks of her as the person who saved him.

He is the best junior clerk in the hall and the most frightened. He could skim more than anyone and has never taken a copper — and is ashamed of how much he could. In S1 he balances "Account Nine" without knowing what it is, catches the forged note of a rival (Fossett), is promoted to the private office, and discovers that every entry in Account Nine is a street of Ninth Sluice marked "to be reclaimed" (SEC-007). He copies the ledger twice (a clerk's habit), flees, falls into the Lows and is hidden by Pell. He finds the CY 295 Dry Charter signed "T. Kett" — "a common name" — and does not let himself recognise the hand. In E11 he lays out the proof that Ruel poisoned the Helm (SEC-001) like a column of figures.

His handwriting is his father's (SEC-006). Ursel knows it ("His father had a beautiful hand", S1E11).

## Love lines (Revision r7)
*Age gate: Hollis 26, Maren 19 in S1 — both adults; by the lead's choice **no romantic beat in S1**, seeds only; feeling from S2. TV 16+.*

### L2 — "The Clerk and the Admiral" (with Maren Corvane, CH-0006)
- **Why her:** she is the first person of the Steps who checks his sum — and finds nothing to correct. He is the only person who never tells her what she wants to hear; that is his heroism ("tells the truth when a lie would pay") turned into a way of loving.
- **Why hidden:** charity clerk, fugitive, purser under a pardon, then her spy in Odo's treasury — every one of his positions dies with a known tie to her.
- **Key beats:** S1E7 *"You could sell this to Fennick for a house on the Steps." — "I would have to keep the house dry."* (seed) · S1E11 *"You could have burned it." — "Twice, by now."* (seed: he copies twice by habit; the double copy is the first gift) · S2 ledger letters by Undertow boat — the entries grow a second column that is not accounts · S2E11 the first letter with no columns: the names of her father's killers · S3 purser of Corvane; the Whitlow powder fraud — her mother's family — proven on his word (**S3E6**, r8: the flag-captains stripped of their flags — dismissed, not hanged) · S4E12 the first kiss and, in the same scene, her order to go into Odo's treasury · S5 her man in the enemy's treasury; E11 he kills Odo; E12 *"I never asked you." — "No. You never do."* · S6E7 she asks him to cut **one page** from the Account Nine file (her father's sight-draft). He will not — SEC-001 goes public by his hand · S6E8 he learns what his father was, and what his father was to Ursel · S6E9 the fire; their last words were a quarrel. After S6E9: sealed.
- **What it costs him:** each season the truth he tells costs him more of her. S6E7 is the price at its highest: he chooses the column over the woman — exactly the choice his father did not make.

### A — "A Beautiful Hand": his father and Dame Ursel (backstory CY 291–296)
Teodor Kett and Ursel were lovers after both were widowed (her husband died CY 291; Hollis's mother, by this dossier's proposal, the same year). Teodor forged the Dry Charter (CY 295) **because she asked**; a year later the bank needed the forger gone and she chose the bank (SEC-006). She raised his son — and her kindness to Hollis was never a mask. Plants: the brass **ledger-clip** she gives him in S1E1 was Teodor's (he picks it up "as one picks up something warm"); S1E11 *"His father had a beautiful hand."*; S4E6 the letter that begins *"U. —"*, put away unread. **S6E8:** he sees his own story written a generation early — a clerk who forged for a ruling woman, and what it bought him — and he has already refused, the night before, to cut a page for his.

### How the lines change his speech and behaviour
- **Speech:** the ledger metaphors stay, but with Maren they become the language of feeling — "carried forward", "it doesn't balance", "received". He speeds up when frightened and **slows down before courage**: with Maren he is slow. He never says a number he has not checked — and he never says a feeling he has not checked either (proposal: the first unchecked sentence of his life is the S2E11 letter with no columns).
- **Behaviour:** the second copy is his love token (S1E6–E11); the clip on every copy (S2–S4) is, unknown to him, a lover's keepsake of another love. He refuses to forge, cut or burn for her — the one thing she asks that he will not do.
- **Staging:** paper, hands, letters `[VO]`; no added `[LIPSYNC-CU]` in S1.
