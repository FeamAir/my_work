## Look (story-level, for the art team)
- **Age band and build:** woman of 54; heavy, slow, seated more than standing; powerful arms.
- **Silhouette, body and bearing:** **heavy and broad**, sits more than she stands — her **knees were ruined on the treadmill** in childhood and she walks with **two sticks** (short distances only); weathered dark-olive skin; clouded eyes. Her weight and her lameness are her history, not her character: she is the quickest mind on the water. Silhouette: a wide seated shape under a hook-ringed hat.
- **Hair:** **salt-white stubble**, cropped to the skull, under a **wide black oilskin hat whose brim is ringed with old fish-hooks**.
- **Eyes:** **both clouded milk-white** (cataracts) — she sees shapes and light; she listens and smells.
- **Distinctive mark:** a debt-walker's brand — a small ring burned on the back of the LEFT hand (from childhood; proposal).
- **Usual clothes and colours:** **plum (mulberry) gown** under layers of black-dyed net shawls. Main colour: plum.
- **Signature item:** a **string of cork net-floats** she counts like beads while she bargains; from S4E10 the **"book of sold men"** (a tarred notebook) in her lap.
- **Changes:** S9: thinner; the hook-ringed hat is the thing Tibb keeps with the book (proposal).

## Voice (for the sound team)
- **Voice character:** deep, husky contralto; slow; Lows accent at its oldest and thickest; a wet laugh.
- **Speech habits:** prices everything ("what's it worth to you?"); calls everyone "chick" or "duck"; never uses a name she hasn't bought; proverbs of the Lows.
- **Catch-phrase:** "Everything's sold, duck. Even the ones who aren't." · House-style echo: "Calm is bought."
- **Never says:** a child's price; "sorry" — until S4.
- **Sample line:** *"I sell men, chick. Men can say no. Children can't. That's the whole of my law."*
- **Delivery for AI:** seated, hat-brim shading the face — `[MS]`, `[OS]`; `[LIPSYNC-CU]` max 3 per season (main from S4).

## Goals
- **Wants (outer):** to keep the Undertow the only law the Lows can rely on.
- **Needs (inner):** to see that her two rules — sell everyone, protect children — cannot both be kept.
- **Fears:** the treadmills; the Choir's robes on the lanes; her own book.
- **Heroism:** she **gets the children out** — in the end at the cost of her life (S9E5).

## Biography
Born in the Third Sluice (proposal), sold onto a treadmill as a debt-walker at nine for her father's debt; walked it for six years, the brand still on her hand. She came off the wheel by stealing the debt-book of the man who owned her. From that day she has run on two rules: everything is for sale, and no child is. Over thirty years she built the Undertow — the dry-pledge racket, the shard trade, the rumour "mouths", the barge-palace in the Lows. She sells to the Listeners, to the bank, to Corvane sailors and to the Lows; Widow Pruitt informs for her; her fence Tuck deals with the mudlarks.

In S1 her barge is on Pell's road; she buys Pell's salvage and, through Tuck, is the first to hear of a stone that "sings". She is fond of Pell without showing it.

In S4 she learns that one of her own under-bosses, Gant, has been buying "ear-tested" children from Gleaner novices and selling them as debt-walkers — the ear-money (SEC-029). She purges him, and her purge exposes the Gleaners (E6). Her ledger later becomes proof of the ear's true nature (S5E6). In S4E10 the Undertow hires out men, no questions asked — a hiring she never forgives herself (`[SEALED — ask lead]`). From then on she keeps the "book of sold men".
