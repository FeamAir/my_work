## 1. The answer in one paragraph

The Spires were not built by a vanished race. They were **grown by the ordinary people of the delta**, who for four centuries sang together onto living limestone reefs until the stone learned the song. The song of a Spire is a **chorus**: a high part that a few trained voices can carry, and a **low part — the Low Note — that must be sung by many**, so that the cost (the voice) is shared and nobody is taken. House Ashare were elected *Keepers* who conducted the people's chorus; later they made the office hereditary, but kept teaching the Low Note to every child as a lullaby. In **OS 700** nine warden families, led by Ilvane, massacred the people's chorus at Lirenne during the Still Days to take the Spires for themselves. The broken chord drowned Lirenne. The Nine called Queen Neveh mad, outlawed common singing (the Hush Law) and invented the "songblood" — the claim that the ear is inherited — while secretly stealing ear-gifted children from the poor to keep their Cantors supplied. Sung by solo voices, without the low part, every Spire has been **detuning** for 312 years: it compensates by **pulling water from upstream**, which is why the uplands are dying, and it cracks, which is why the delta floods. The last full verse of the Low Note survived through **Oshra**, Neveh's servant girl, and her descendants in the Quiet — down to **Pell**. The saga's resolution is not a hero's solo, but the people singing again.

## 2. The secret behind the narration

The *Chronicle of the Dry Years* is the **official record of the Concord** (Relay logs, Hearings, Choir annals), compiled and read aloud by **Hollis Kett** in old age. Its errors are the official lies — always in favour of the dry. In the epilogue (S12E13) we see the frame: old Hollis, hoarse from the S6 fire, reading the official Chronicle aloud to a room of Lows children and to Pell, voiceless, and then closing the book: *"That is what they wrote. Now — here is what happened."* The whole series was the correction. Part of the official record was forged by Hollis himself (S5 for Odo, S9–S11 as "Quire"). This is why the narrator is wrong in a *consistent* way (see §6).

## 6. The Chronicler's rules (to keep his "lies" fair)

1. He reads the **official record**. He is never wrong about dates, numbers or prices (a clerk's pride) — he is wrong about **causes and motives**, always in favour of the Steps.
2. Known official lies he repeats as fact until corrected on screen: the Builders vanished; Neveh was mad; the ear is in the blood; Still Water was "an act of the sea"; Liss and Teodor drowned by accident; Ione was born to Cressa; the Vanth sold the Stair; the Ossari drought is weather.
3. After each reveal, the next Chronicler line about that topic is **unchanged** (it's the official record) — but the scene that follows contradicts it. Fans learn to hear the gap.
4. His sign-off "…and the water kept its counsel." changes only in S12E13: "…and the water kept its counsel. We did not."
