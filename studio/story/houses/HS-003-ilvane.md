## Public history

**The first teachers.** Ilvane were singing-masters of Lirenne. At the Crowning of the Keepers (~OS 380–420, EV-0051) the Keepers made them wardens of the Pale Spire on its white cliff. The founding Ilvane warden is a figure of the Old Song era (prequel window) — remembered in the cloister as "the Teacher". In the seventh century of the Old Song (OS 600–699, EV-0052) Ilvane founded the **Cantor schools** at Pale Crag — publicly a holy reform: trained Cantors, one voice to a Spire, "the song made worthy". Every warden family sent its singers there.

**The Breaking (OS 700) — the Chronicle's version.** Matriarch **Sereth Ilvane** "led the Nine against the mad Queen" and, after Lirenne sank, gave the realm its new order: one Choir, one law of silence for the untaught (the Hush Law, CY 1), one family to keep the song "true". The tapestry of the Breaking hangs in the Choir temple because Ilvane paid for it.

**Rise and "conquest" — by schools, not swords.** Ilvane never won a battle. It took the song.

**The two branches.** *Ilvane of the Hall* (the lords, the townhouse, the lands) and *Ilvane of the Crag* (the cloister branch — Ilvane-born Cantors and children born to the cloister, entered in the cloister's **Book of the Blood**). Both wear the open hand. The Crag outranks the Hall in the Choir; the Hall outranks the Crag in the Ring — except that the First Cantor speaks for the drowned.

**Marriage.** "Keep the blood true": the Hall marries cousins or its own household. Lady **Cressa** was born a **Pallis** (HS-029), of the cloister stewards who keep the Gleaner rolls (*proposal* for `writer-characters`, who asked for her birth-house).

**Alliances and debts (CY 312).** Controls two votes in practice (Ilvane + the Choir) — but in S1 they split (Aurin for Corvane, the Choir for Fennick). Contempt for Fennick ("clerks"). Needs Lantry's money: the Choir's debt of ~95,000 marks is, in practice, Ilvane's.

## True history

- **SEC-003.** In OS 700 the Nine — led by Ilvane under Sereth, with Corvane ships sealing the harbour — massacred the people's chorus at Lirenne during the Still Days; that broke the Spire and drowned the city. Neveh was not mad. The people used to sing the Spires together; the Hush Law exists to stop that. The Unison Decree of CY 34 is the same act in peacetime: it silenced the last *group* singing that was not Ilvane's. (Revealed S5E10.)
- **EV-0052, the Cantor schools (canon truth).** The schools trained *solo* Cantors so that the warden families could sing a Spire **without crowds** — "the solo heresy". The Unison Decree of CY 34 finished the work inside the Concord.
- **SEC-005.** The "songblood" is a lie: the ear is taught in early childhood, and only by the forbidden Lows lullaby hummed to a child before about seven. Ilvane and the Choir keep their ranks full by **gleaning** ear-gifted children from the poor and passing some of them off as Ilvane-born. Numbers (for the writers' room, never on screen as a table): of the 31 Cantors recorded as Ilvane-born between CY 250 and CY 312, **at least 19 were gleaned**; of the 12 who were truly Hall-born, only 3 ever made a Spire answer, and all 3 had Lows wet-nurses (*proposal* — it follows from the r2 rule "only the lullaby gives the ear"; lead to confirm). (Revealed S5E6.) *Whether the Book of the Blood's "of the Crag" entries are the device used for this is a question to the lead* (report Q-05) — it would touch Vesna's sealed entry, so this file does not say so.
- **SEC-004 (as it touches the tree).** Ione is not Cressa's daughter. Cressa's own daughter **Ilse** (CH-0068) died in CY 294 (EV-0257; the infant grave at Pale Crag is the S2 clue). Ione is **Ama**, a gleaned Ninth Sluice child (true mother Liss, CH-0023), taken by Sister Venn on the Night of Still Water (CY 295) and installed in the Hall nursery. Aurin knew she was "brought in". (Revealed S4E10.)
- **SEC-029.** The Gleaners are paid by the head ("ear-money"); novices pad the count with children who have no ear and sell those as debt-walkers through an Undertow under-boss. The cloister stewards (Pallis, HS-029) keep two rolls — the **White Roll** (foundlings "blessed by the Lady", as shown to the world) and the **Ash Roll** (true origin, price paid, and what became of each child). The Ash Roll — kept by Cressa's own birth family — is what Aurin gives Cressa in S5. (Revealed S4E6.)
- **The shortage (r2).** Because the ear comes only from the lullaby, and the Hush Law — enforced far more efficiently since Fennick's Listeners took the Lows in CY 251 — has frightened Lows mothers out of humming, the Choir is starving itself. Numbers: `HS-090-choir.md`, Cantor census.

## Culture and look

- **Faith:** Ilvane *are* the Choir's piety: silence before meals, the hand over the mouth at the Still Days rite (the Lows copy it), the "open hand" greeting (palm out, fingers spread — "I hold no song back").
- **Hair:** the house is "silver-haired by birth" **(belief)**. Every Cantor of the Crag takes the weekly **Cantor's rinse**, publicly a purification ("the water of the Crag on the head that sings"). Art note: Ione's dark roots in S3–S4 captivity.
- **Dress:** white and pale-violet layered robes, long sleeves that cover the hands when not signing; violet silk throat-bands for Cantors (black when the voice is lost); silver rings on the signing hand for senior mutes. Lords of the Hall wear white coats with violet lining — never a weapon.
- **Sign:** Ilvane invented the Cantors' hand-sign for mute singers; Vesna's signs are voiced by her "Voice" (Brother Elm). Sign is *formal* at Pale Crag and *fast* in the Lows (the Lows learned it from Cantors' servants — usable in S2 when Ione learns street sign).
- **Architecture:** Pale Crag — a white cliff-cloister of terraces and covered stairs around the Pale Spire; long galleries with open arches facing the sea; the novices' dormitory under the Spire; the crypt of infant graves (S2 clue). In Highwater, the Ilvane townhouse: white plaster, violet shutters, a silent courtyard.
- **Household:** cloister stewards (Pallis), the White Staves (cloister guard, ~400, carry staves not swords), Gleaners (Choir officers, see HS-090).

### The house scheme on three floors — priority songs, gleaning, ear-money
| Floor | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Steps — invents** | the First Cantor and the Choir chapter | In a storm a Spire cannot hold everything in sight; Cantors choose where to turn the water. The Choir sells **priority songs** — a written promise that a district, a ship or a warehouse is sung first — to whoever pays (houses, merchants, the bank). To pay the bank, it also needs ever more Cantors → **gleaning**. |
| **Mids — executes** | Gleaner novices and officers, the Pallis stewards, the Cantors themselves | Gleaners walk the Lows "testing" children (S1E5); stewards keep the White and Ash Rolls; Cantors on the crown follow the priority sheet, not the map. |
| **Lows — pays** | Lows mothers and children; districts that cannot buy priority | Children vanish "into the Lady's blessing"; the ones without the ear come back as debt-walkers (Patch, CH-0064); unpaid districts are sung last — Ninth Sluice in CY 295 and CY 312. |

*Upward consequence:* the Choir's debt makes it **vote Fennick in S1** ("the Choir owes the bank its roof", S1E7–E8); the Cantor shortage worsens every year — by S5 some Spires are sung in shifts by children, by **S6 great houses must hire singers from the Lows** (the Hum), which is how Pell buys Maren's election.

## Notes for production

- **Five recognisers:** white + pale violet (+ silver); the open hand holding a spiral stone; layered robes with hand-covering sleeves and throat-bands; white terraces and arched galleries of Pale Crag; a single held unison on E that never harmonises.
- **Sigil drawing:** a white open right hand, palm toward the viewer, fingers slightly spread; a small grey spiral stone (spiral clockwise, three turns) resting in the palm; field pale violet. *Caution:* the spiral is also the Lows/Ashare sign — on the Ilvane sigil it is **always held in a hand** (owned), never free.
- **Colour cautions:** white is Ilvane's; the Choir is *undyed linen* (warm off-white, coarse texture) — keep the two distinguishable: Ilvane white is cool and silky, the Choir's linen warm and matte.
- **Faces:** Vesna is always veiled (no face reference); many Cantors can be shown from behind at the crown, hooded.
