## What it is

The official faith and the order of Cantors. It venerates the **First Voices** (the Builders, **belief**) and the Spires as their gift; every Cantor is a priest; the **First Cantor** heads the order, is elected for life by the **chapter** (all Cantors who still have a voice, plus the mute seniors who "keep the chair") and, by ancient custom, **speaks for the drowned** — casts the Sunken Vote in a tie (RULE-009).

The Choir is a voting power because in CY 1 Ilvane gave it the **Bell Spire** of Highwater (renamed the Choir Spire). Since the **Unison Decree** (CY 34) no Cantor may be trained anywhere but Pale Crag, so **every Spire in the realm is sung by Cantors the Choir rents out** — to the Helm (Crown Spire), to the houses, even to Fennick.

## The Gleaners

Choir officers and novices who walk the Lows to find children "blessed by the Lady" — children who make an echo-stone hum. Found children go to Pale Crag "for testing" before about seven; those who "take the ear" are entered as foundling novices (the **White Roll**); the stewards (Pallis, HS-029) keep the real record (the **Ash Roll**).

- **Ear-money (SEC-029, revealed S4E6):** a Gleaner is paid **3 marks a head** for each child brought for testing and **10 more** if the child takes the ear. Novices pad the count with children who have no ear; those are "returned to the Lady" — in fact sold for **2 marks** each as debt-walkers through an Undertow under-boss. Patch (CH-0064) is one.
- **The songblood (SEC-005, revealed S5E6):** some gleaned children are passed off as Ilvane-born. The ear is taught, not inherited, and only by the forbidden Lows lullaby hummed before about seven — so the Choir **cannot make its own singers**.

## Cantor census — the shortage (SEC-005, r2)

Voiced Cantors serving each Spire (mute seniors not counted). A Spire is "safe" with about 8 (daily tide-songs + a rested reserve for a storm); below 5 it detunes faster; below 3 it is sung in shifts and by children.

**Why (writers' room only):** a Cantor's voice lasts ~15–20 years of service (less with Great Songs). Intake has fallen since CY 251, when Fennick's Listeners took over the Highwater Lows and the Hush Law began to be enforced street by street: mothers stopped humming; the Quiet hid their children harder. Weaker Spires need more Great Songs, which burn voices faster — a spiral. The Choir blames "thin blood in the age".

*Upward consequence:* S1E8 — Ione counts how few they are at the retuning; S5 — two Spires sung in shifts by exhausted children; **S6 — great houses with failing Spires must hire singers from the Lows** (the Hum) — Pell's leverage, Maren's election.

## Culture and look

- **Dress:** undyed linen robes (warm off-white, coarse), rope belts, hoods; Cantors add a violet throat-band (Ilvane colour, black when voiceless). Gleaners: linen with a black stole and a small echo-stone on a chain (to test children). Novices: short linen tunics, shaved napes.
- **Rites:** daily tide-songs at the crown; the **Still Days retuning** (`power_and_money.md` §8) — the order's great rite; at Still Days commoners kneel with hands over mouths.
- **Temple:** the Choir Spire rises out of a round temple in Highwater's Steps; the Breaking tapestry hangs in its ambulatory; the roof is new copper (the bank's).

## Notes for production

- **Five recognisers:** undyed linen + black; nine strokes over a level line; hooded linen robes with violet throat-bands; round temple at the Spire's foot, the tapestry; the passed-on unison on G.
- **Caution:** keep the Choir's warm linen distinct from Ilvane's cool white silk (`HS-003-ilvane.md`).
- **Crowds:** hooded Cantors from behind at the crown; novices in rows with shaved napes (no faces needed).
