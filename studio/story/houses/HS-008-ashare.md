> Note for the keeper: `extinct: OS 700-11` = the Still Days of OS 700 (the Breaking, EV-0100). The Chronicle's reckoning ("Old Song") counts from the first Keeper (OS 1). The Sunken Vote (RULE-009) is the legal ghost of this house.

## Public history (the Chronicle's version — to be used in all public material)

- **The Keepers (OS 1 – ~400).** In OS 1 the first Keeper "of House Ashare took the song of Lirenne" — the Spire of the great harbour-city at the edge of the Sunk, then dry land. The Chronicle calls every Keeper a **Tide-King** from that date and lists thirty-one of them. Under the Keepers, Lirenne became the first city of the delta: its Spire the greatest, its harbour the hinge of all trade, its singers famous.
- **The Crowning of the Keepers (~OS 380–420, prequel window P2).** The Keepership became hereditary in House Ashare under **Isembar Ashare**; nine Spire-warden families were sworn to the crown (Corvane, Dask, Ilvane, Quell, Harrow, Vanth as Spire-wardens; Rusk, Moll, Ostry as sword-families). *Name only — the events belong to P2.*
- **The Tide-Kings (OS ~400 – 700).** Three centuries of a crowned house: the "Pearl Court", the teal banners, the spiral shell as the royal sign, the Still Days as a royal festival when "the whole city came to the harbour to hear the Spires retuned".
- **The Breaking (OS 700, Still Days, prequel window P3).** Queen **Neveh Ashare**, "the Sunken Queen", went mad and sang Lirenne into the sea. The nine warden families rose, killed her, and founded the Concord (CY 1). The Chronicler: *"She sang her city into the sea."* The line of the Tide-Kings ended with her; the Spire went silent under the water; the ninth voice of the realm was set aside "for whoever holds Lirenne" — the Sunken Vote.
- **What survives.** The spiral shell became the folk sign of the Lady Below in the Lows; the Breaking tapestry in the Choir temple; a few teal-glazed tiles dredged by Quell salvors; the Corvane heirloom pearl, "won at Lirenne".

## True history

- **SEC-003.** Neveh was not mad. The people of Lirenne used to **sing the Spires together** — the Still Days were a people's chorus, not a royal rite. In OS 700 the Nine warden families, **led by Ilvane, with Corvane ships sealing the harbour**, massacred the people's chorus at Lirenne during the Still Days; that broke the Spire and drowned the city. The Hush Law (CY 1) exists to stop the people from ever singing together again. A servant girl, **Oshra** (CH-0031), escaped carrying the last verse — Pell's ancestress. Revealed S5E10. What the Queen herself sang in the last hour, and why exactly the city sank, is `[SEALED — ask lead]`.
- **The Keepers were elected.** Before Isembar, Keepers were chosen by the singers of Lirenne, and many were not of the Ashare family; the Chronicle backdates the crown and the house to OS 1 (consistent with the Chronicler never stating a false fact: he says the Keepers were "of Lirenne", and calls them Tide-Kings — a title, not a lineage). This is P2's ground; no new secret.
- **The Builders.** Public: "the Builders, a vanished race", the "First Voices" of the Choir. True: `[SEALED — ask lead]`.
- **The Ashare blood after OS 700.** Official: extinct. Whether any child of the house survived is `[SEALED — ask lead]`; the Pearl Pretender's claim in S6 is false (SEC-016) — that is all this file states.

## Culture and look (for flashbacks, the tapestry, the Pretender's imitation, and prequels)

- **Lirenne:** a harbour-city of pale stone and teal-glazed tile on a ring of islands around its Spire; arcades over canals; pearl-divers' steps; the Spire taller than any now standing, with a crown of many throats.
- **Dress:** pearl-grey and teal silks, loose and layered; shell ornaments; no gloves — hands shown open; the royal sign a silver spiral shell worn at the throat.
- **Customs:** the Still Days as a festival on the harbour, the whole city on the quays (public belief: "to hear the Spires retuned"; what the crowd itself did is SEC-003 and is shown only in S5E10 and the prequels).
- **The Pretender's imitation (S6):** teal banners, a spiral shell badge, a pearl on a cord — brokers' guesses. Art should make the imitation subtly *wrong* (the spiral drawn the wrong way — Lio "draws it the wrong way", S6E3; too-bright teal; brand-new silk).

## Power and money (as it was)

Lirenne held the sea-gate of the whole delta: harbour dues, pearls, the first Spire. The Tide-Kings kept no great army — the warden families were their swords and ships. After OS 700: nothing but the Sunken Vote, the Sunk Charter (Quell's salvage, CY 3), and legends of "the Tide-Kings' vaults" (which the salvage bonds sell in S6).
