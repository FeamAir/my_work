## How to read this script

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SC n — INT./EXT. PL-xxxx PLACE — TIME [start–end]` | scene heading: canon place ID, time of day, timecode inside the episode |
| `[VO]` | voice over picture; the speaker is not in the scene |
| `[OS]` | off screen: the speaker is in the scene, not in the frame (or seen from behind) |
| `[MS]` | mid-shot or wider; mouth small in frame or three-quarters turned. Prefer cutting to the listener |
| `[LIPSYNC-CU]` | close-up with synchronous speech. **Five in the episode, no more:** Sc 4 Nan · Sc 11 Ruel · Sc 12 the Helm · Sc 15 Ursel · Sc 20 Wick |
| `(…)` after the name | emotion and delivery for the voice |
| **FIXED** | the wording is binding. No paraphrase, no trim in the edit |
| `[ECHO]` | the line is quoted or recalled in a later episode. Never cut, never reworded |
| `SOUND:` `MUSIC:` `SILENCE:` | cues for the sound director and the composer |
| `SDH:` | descriptive subtitle that must exist in the upload and be burned into vertical cuts |
| `♪` | the five-note lullaby. **Heard only in Sc 1, Sc 3 and Sc 20.** Nowhere else, in no arrangement |
| `(bk)` | back, hands or silhouette only; no face |
| `LA` | the scene is carried by limited animation (stills, camera move, parallax, light) |

**Rules that hold in every scene** (*fixed — ask the lead*):
- Every braid named here is on the **left**. Ruel's ring and his clasp are **left-handed**. Nan's one earring is **left**.
- **The stone and the ring never share a frame**, a split screen, or a cut that sets one beside the other. In this running order the stone's scenes and the ring's scenes are never adjacent (a bank scene always stands between them).
- The stone never glows. Its sound is *seen* as a tremor and as rings on water. Glow belongs to the Spires.
- No one sings in the Lows. Whistles, plank-stamps, a count spoken on one pitch. Wick's one sung note in Sc 4 is the only one.
- The low register of the score stays empty everywhere, so that the stone's note has a place nothing else has used.
- No shot holds more than four readable faces.
- Names spoken aloud in this episode: Pell, Wick, Nan, Mags (once), Ione, Ruel, Hollis, Kett, Dame Lantry, Lucan (twice), Queen Neveh (once, the Chronicler). **Nobody else is named aloud.** The reedmaster, the taster, the clerk at the next desk, the lord in the relay room and the tithe-collector keep their faces, costumes and lines; their names come in E2–E3.

**The stone's sound (for the sound director; applies to Sc 3 and Sc 20 and to every cut made from them):** one low sustained tone, below every other pitch in the episode, felt as much as heard, **plus a fine glassy upper partial, a thin "itch" riding on it, that a phone speaker reproduces.** On large speakers the low tone leads; on a phone the upper partial carries the answer. The rings on the water start on the same frame as the sound.

## SC 1 — EXT. PL-0008 NINTH SLUICE LANE → PL-0021 EVACUATION GATE → OPEN FLOOD WATER — NIGHT, CY 295 [0:00–1:10]

*No dialogue. No score until the hum. The first frame of the upload is image 1.*

SOUND (from frame one, under everything to 0:30): black water moving fast; **long single whistles** passed from roof to roof, the Lows' call for help, near, then far, then farther; **an iron bar beaten on iron**, somebody's alarm, ragged, not a bell. Rain on water. No music.

**1. THE CRADLE [0:00–0:06].** A flooded street at night, water to the door-lintels. A wicker cradle comes **fast** down the middle of it on the current, spinning, knocking once against a post where a lantern still burns. The lantern throws a hard warm rim on the wicker: the cradle reads as a cradle in half a second, on a phone, with the sound off.
[0:03] TOP SHOT, straight down into it as it turns under the lantern: **it is empty.** A soaked blanket, pushed back. Nothing else. (No knot, cord or ribbon anywhere on this cradle. *Fixed.*)
SDH: `[whistles for help, roof to roof]` `[iron beaten on iron]`

**2. THE WATER [0:06–0:10].** One shot. Along the plank-walks the lanterns go out from the far end toward us, one, two, three, as the water takes them. In the foreground a doorpost with a ladder of old tide-marks cut into it and, burned into the wood beside them, the numeral **IX**. The water climbs the marks, passes the highest, and covers the numeral.

**3. THE GATE [0:10–0:30].**
[0:10] HIGH ANGLE over a great wall with one gate in it: the whole wrong in one frame. **On this side**, water to the waist and a packed crowd, lanterns held over heads, all pressing one way. **On the far side**, dry stone steps going up, lit, almost empty; a few helmets.
[0:14] From the dry side. A pair of **young hands** in soldier's cuffs (bk, no face; rain-wet, lit from behind so that they are shape more than detail) take hold of the gate bar and **drag it across.**
[0:22] In the narrowing gap, at the height of the bar: **one pair of eyes**, lantern-lit, looking straight through at the hands. Not a whole face. Then the gate is shut and the eyes are gone.
[0:25] The bar drops into its iron. Fists on the gate from the wrong side, many, then a great many.
SOUND: the bar going home is the loudest sound so far. Voices without words behind the iron.
SDH: `[the bar drops]` `[fists on the gate]`

**4. THE HUM [0:30–0:45].**
[0:30] CUT under water. Black. SILENCE, two seconds, no more.
[0:32] The first music of the series comes **through** the water and brings us up with it: a woman's voice, unaccompanied, mouth closed, **five notes ♪**.
A roof-ridge above the flood. A woman's silhouette against the rain-light (bk): slight, one long braid on the **left**. She hums the five notes out over the water, and waits, and hums them again.
MUSIC: the voice alone. No instrument joins. The whistles have stopped.
SDH: `[a woman hums five notes]`

**5. THE BOAT [0:45–0:56].** A rescue boat with one lantern at the bow, low in the water, crowded with wrapped shapes. Among them a baby, about a year old, sitting up in a blanket, lantern-light on a wet cheek and nothing more of the face. The woman's five notes come across the water. The baby hums the first **two** of them back, wavering, not quite true.
SDH: `[the baby hums two notes back]`

**6. THE SLEEVE [0:56–1:03].** A grey robed sleeve and a small, clean hand (bk) reach into the lantern-light and lift the baby out of frame. The blanket trails and is gone.
On the roof, far off now, the woman begins the five notes again. Nobody answers.

**7. THE LANTERN [1:03–1:10].** The boat's lantern goes on without the child, small on the black water, past a doorpost of which only the top still shows.
SUPER, over the image, no cut to black: ***The Night of Still Water.***
MUSIC: the hum stops in the middle of the phrase.

## SC 2 — EXT. PL-0008 NINTH SLUICE LANE, OUTSIDE PL-0019 NAN'S STILT-HOUSE — DAWN, CY 312, LONGDARK 6 [1:10–2:00]

The same doorpost in grey daylight, dry. The ladder of tide-marks; one mark far above all the others; the burned **IX**. Where the water was there is mud, and a stilt-lane on its legs above it.
STAMP, lower corner: ***Seventeen years later.***

SOUND: gulls; a wheel creaking somewhere overhead; a spoken step-count on one pitch, far off; and under the whole district a tower's hum that is audibly **flat and thin**. No music.

A fist knocks on the doorpost, on the highest mark.

THE TITHE-COLLECTOR (light ref): slate tabard with a sulphur stripe, a money-box chained to his wrist, a slate under his arm. He does not look at the house. He looks at the nets drying on its rail.

COLLECTOR [MS] (bored, official, reading it off the slate)
Ninth Sluice, fourth lane. Nan's house. Two months owing.
(he rattles the box, once)
Six coppers by dusk. Or I take the nets.

A boy drops into frame **upside down**: WICK, eleven, hanging by his knees from the rope-bridge above the door. Skinny, gap-toothed, a mouse-brown mop with a tuft at the right temple, an oatmeal jumper sliding toward his chin.

WICK [MS] (cheerful, quick, thick Lows)
Nan's drowned.

COLLECTOR [OS] (not looking up)
She drowned last month.

WICK
Drowned again. Took the money with her. Both times.

NAN [OS] (inside the house; thin, cracked, very loud)
I'll drown *you*, you little eel! Get down off that bridge!

The collector writes one stroke on his slate.

COLLECTOR [MS]
Dusk.

He goes on to the next door. Knock.

PELL comes out past the nets: fifteen, short, wiry, leaning forward as if into wind. Tar-brown oilskin; cropped rust-brown hair; one long thin braid on the **left** with a blue tin tag stamped **IX**. A long-handled rake over one shoulder, a coil of rope over the other. She watches the collector's back for exactly as long as it takes to count something.

WICK [OS] (righting himself, dropping to the planks)
Six! We never found six in a *week*.

Pell looks up.

THE RISE (six seconds, once; a tall still, vertical move; do not extend): with her glance the camera climbs out of the lane. The stilt-town standing in its own shadow, **below** the line of the sea. Above it the long brick wall of the dike. Above that, terraces of pale granite, banners. And over all of it, black towers with spiral grooves, one of them, the nearest, giving off the thin flat hum we have been hearing. Then straight back down to her face.

Below the plank-walk the canal has no water in it. Silt, open to the sky, as far as the bend.

PELL [MS] (dry, clipped, already moving) **FIXED**
You want it found, you pay the mud. Mud doesn't do favours.

She is over the edge and down the ladder. Wick looks at the drop, looks at the ladder, and jumps.

## SC 3 — EXT. PL-0020 NINTH SLUICE BACK-CANAL, BY THE OLD SLUICE — DAWN EBB [2:00–4:00]

Mud flats between stilt-legs, shining. Four or five small figures far apart, each bent over a rake in a lane of its own.

MAGS (light ref; never closer than a full-body long shot): tall for fifteen, gangly, a red rag in black frizz, a man's waistcoat all pockets. Two lanes over, the loudest laugh on the canal.

MAGS [OS] (hoarse, cackling, across the flats)
Nothing left in Ninth Sluice but buttons, Pell!

WICK [MS] (hands cupped)
Count your own, Mags!

Pell has not stopped walking. She is looking at something ahead that she has never seen.

[2:14] THE OLD SLUICE. A stone sill, green-black, running across the canal bed, **bare**, with the ebb gone out from it for the first time in anyone's life. Past the sill the ground drops away into a sump: a pool of standing water, perfectly black, perfectly still.

WICK [OS] (quieter)
The old sill. Pell. You can see the *stone*.

Pell stops one pace short of the sill. Her face is not the face from the lane. She unslings the rope, ties it round her own waist, hard, and puts the other end into Wick's two fists.

WICK [MS]
It's only water.

PELL [MS] (not looking at it)
Hold the rope.

She will not step past the sill. She kneels on it and works the pool with the rake at arm's length. The rake comes up with black weed. Again. Weed. Wick has the rope wrapped twice round his wrists and his heels dug in.

[2:42] The rake snags on something that does not give. To free it she has to lean out, over the water, the rope taut behind her.
CLOSE on Pell, silent (no lip-sync): jaw set, eyes wide, breath short. Her reflection comes up to meet her out of the black.

[2:48] Without knowing she does it, mouth shut, she **hums. Three notes ♪**: breathy, frightened, not pretty.
SDH: `[she hums, three notes]`

WICK [OS] (a hiss)
Pell. *Mouth.*

His eyes go up and to the left: over the roofs, the black tower. She stops. She had not heard herself.

[3:00] The rake comes free. On its tines a rotted cord, and on the cord **a black stone no bigger than a thumbnail, glassy, cracked across.**
As it breaks the surface **it hums back.**
SOUND: the stone's tone (see the note at the head of this file): low, sustained, under the floor of the ear, with the thin glassy partial riding on it. Two seconds.
The whole black pool shivers into **rings**, edge to edge, on the same frame as the sound.
SILENCE around it: no gulls, no wheel.
SDH: `[a low hum answers]`

Pell is hauling herself back onto the sill, the rope biting, her own heart in her ears. She has heard nothing.
Wick has seen all of it. He looks from the rings to her back. For once there is nothing in his mouth.

She sits on the sill with her feet on the dry side and turns the thing over on her palm. Wet, black, a crack like a hair across its face.

WICK [MS] (small)
It sang.

PELL [MS] (flat; weighing it in her hand)
Stones don't sing.
(she closes her fist on it)
That's six coppers, that is.

MAGS [OS] (far off)
What've you got, then?

Pell's fist is already inside her oilskin.

PELL [MS] (calling back, bored)
Buttons!

Mags's laugh comes over the mud. She has not left her lane. She has seen a girl sit down on a sill.

Wick is still looking at the pool. The last ring reaches the edge and is gone.

SOUND: a two-finger whistle from the plank-walks, **rising three**: all clear, tide turning. The gulls come back.

They climb.

## SC 4 — INT. PL-0019 NAN'S STILT-HOUSE — MORNING [4:00–5:20]

One room on legs. A table, a shelf, a clay water-bowl, a window of oiled cloth. On the floor by the wall a net-chest with rope handles, old, plain, the sort of thing nobody looks at.

NAN mends net at the table and does not look up: tiny, hunched, an indigo headscarf with white wisps escaping it, a crooked right little finger working the needle. One spiral-shell earring, **left**. (Nobody looks at the earring. The camera does not stop on it.)

Silt-bread on a board, cut in three. Pell eats with one hand. The other is a fist in her lap.

NAN [MS] (quick, scolding, without a breath)
Fifteen years old and you still track silt across my floor. Sit. Eat. And don't tell me what you found, I can see from here it was nothing.

Pell slides her own half-eaten piece across the board to Wick, without looking at him. He takes it without thanks. It is an old arrangement.

WICK [MS] (mouth full, bursting)
Nan, the old sill's bare. Right down to the stone. And Pell,

Under the table, Pell's foot finds his shin.

WICK (CONT'D)
— Pell found buttons.

The needle stops.

NAN [MS]
The old sill. Bare.
(to herself)
Not in my floods. Not in Ninth Sluice since —

She bites the thread through instead of finishing, and knots it.

SOUND: through the wall, the Sluice Spire's morning song begins: one trained voice far above them, thin, and **flat**.

Wick, chewing, cheerful, joins it. **One loud sung note.**

NAN'S HAND IS OVER HIS MOUTH before the note is finished. (The one generated clip of the scene.)

SILENCE in the room, three seconds. Nobody moves. Pell's fist on the table. Wick's eyes above the old hand. Outside, the Spire sings on, thin and flat.

NAN [LIPSYNC-CU] (very quiet; the scold gone out of the voice; what is left is fear) **FIXED**
Never sing, chick. Hum if you must, and only for the house.

She takes her hand away. Wick does not make a joke.

Nan shoulders a bale of mended net bigger than she is.

NAN [MS → OS] (loud again, at the door)
Chandler on the dike pays today. I'm back by dusk. And if that tithe-man comes early, you sit on the chest.

The door. Her feet on the ladder, small and quick, going down.

Pell opens her fist. The stone has printed its shape in her palm.

WICK [OS] (a whisper)
It answered you.

Pell knots the rotted cord round her own neck and drops the stone inside her oilskin.

PELL [MS]
It's dusk's money. That's all it is.

CLOSE: the stone against her skin, black, cracked, catching the window.

MATCH CUT (black stone → a black tower woven in thread).
SOUND: a dry page turns.

## SC 5 — THE BREAKING TAPESTRY · TITLE — NO TIME (THE CHRONICLE) `LA` [5:20–6:14]

*Document sequence. Woven textile, flat, a style of its own: this is the look of every later "Chronicle" sequence. Camera moves only; nothing moves inside the weave. Six panels, about seven seconds each. The tapestry hangs in PL-0013; we see details, never the room.*

MUSIC: a drone and one dry bowed line, "archival". No low register. No lullaby.

THE CHRONICLER is heard for the first time: old, dry, precise, faintly hoarse; a record-keeper reading entries. About 110 words a minute. No feeling at the third sentence. *Every line of his in this script is **FIXED**: delivered exactly as written.*

PANEL 1. The black tower in thread, rising from a woven sea. The camera comes down it to its foot: a QUEEN singing, a spiral-shell circlet, pearl and teal; and **behind her, crowds**, row on row, every small mouth open. In the border, spirals, every one turning **sunwise**.

CHRONICLER [VO] **FIXED**
The First Voices raised the Spires. They are not heard now.

PANEL 2. Nine banners on a shore; nine families under them, facing the tower.

CHRONICLER [VO] **FIXED**
In the last year of the old kingdom, the nine warden families found Queen Neveh past reason, and her court with her.

PANEL 3. Close in the weave: the Queen's singing hand. On it **a black pearl.**

PANEL 4. The next panel: a servant girl half hidden by a pillar. A braid on the **left**. A spiral shell held against her chest in one hand. Her other hand is a **closed fist.**

PANEL 5. A sail with a cormorant on it, drawn across the harbour mouth. Ships at sea.

PANEL 6. The Queen **alone at the crown** of the tower, small against it, singing. Below her, the crowd of panel 1 has fallen: small shapes lying in the weave. Behind her the sea is standing up.

CHRONICLER [VO] **FIXED** `[ECHO]`
She sang her city into the sea.

The camera draws back until the six panels are one dim cloth.

CHRONICLER [VO] **FIXED** `[ECHO]`
The Nine made their Concord on what stayed dry. This is the three hundred and twelfth year of that Concord: three hundred and twelve years, each with its tithe, and each balance carried forward.
(the smallest pause)
…and the water kept its counsel.

The last panel's black tower fills the frame.

## SC 6 — EXT. PL-0008 THE NINTH SLUICE TREADMILL PUMP — LATE MORNING `LA` [6:14–7:14]

Three great wooden wheels in a row over a sump, each taller than a house. Inside each, twenty backs walking uphill and getting nowhere. Two wheels turn. **The third stands still.** Nobody is looking at it.

SOUND: the walkers' count, spoken on **one pitch**, never a second note: *"…and four. And five. And six…"* Wood groaning. Water lifted and dropped. A reed whistle marking the step.

THE REEDMASTER (light ref) walks the nearest wheel at its rim: barrel body, huge thighs, sun-black skin, a grey beard twisted into a single rope. No left hand. A reed whistle in the right. He speaks a sentence to a step.

Pell holds a knotted rag up to him. In it: buttons, a bent spoon, a buckle.

PELL [MS]
Four coppers of that, if the rag-man's drunk. I want a half-walk for the other two.

REEDMASTER [MS] (bass; slow; a step, a sentence)
No half-walks today.
(step)
Short a pin. Two wheels. Half the walks.
(step) **FIXED**
A wheel doesn't care who walks it.
(step)
I've forty grown backs it doesn't care about, ahead of you.

WICK [MS] (helpful)
So sing it dry. Like they do up the hill.

A dozen walkers turn their heads at the word. The count stumbles.
The reed whistle, once, **sharp**. The count resumes. Wick finds his feet very interesting.

REEDMASTER [MS] (lower, to Pell; not unkind)
Reading's today. The Helm reads the map, up at the palace.
(step)
Whoever reads that map decides who gets wet.
(step)
Come dusk we'll all know what Ninth Sluice is worth.

He does not say it like a man who expects good news.

SOUND: from far over the roofs, a whistle: **falling, two tones.** Passed on, nearer. Passed on again, nearer still, then going away down the canal.

The count does not break. But every head on the wheel goes down, and a walker's hand closes over something at his belt.

REEDMASTER [OS] (not moving his lips much)
Listeners.

CLOSE: Pell's hand is flat against her collar, on the cord, before she has decided to move it. Wick sees the hand. She takes it away.

The whistle fades. The third wheel stands still.

MATCH CUT (the turning hub → a compass rose in ink).

## SC 7 — INT. PL-0078 THE MAP OFFICE, TABLE-TOP ONLY — MIDDAY `LA` [7:14–7:54]

*Document sequence on the Tithe Map itself: the season's map. Legible, designed, kept as a master file.*

A great sheet under a cartographer's hands (bk; Inchley livery cuffs). The delta and its many mouths. HIGHWATER: the Steps, the Mids, the Lows below the dike. Nine black towers, each ringed in its holder's colour. As the Chronicler speaks, tints wash in over the districts: a strong tint, a middling tint, a thin tint.

MUSIC: the drone of Sc 5, lighter. No theme.

CHRONICLER [VO] **FIXED**
The Tithe Map sets down who is sung first when the storm comes, and who after. First Song. Second Song. Last Song.

In the legend, three labelled tints. Below them a fourth: **a pale wash**, its label under the cartographer's thumb.

CHRONICLER [VO] **FIXED**
Of the nine Spires, eight still answered. One of the eight answered badly.

On the map one tower's ring is drawn thinner than the rest: the one above the lowest districts.

CHRONICLER [VO] **FIXED** `[ECHO]`
By law the Helm reads the map aloud, and signs it; and the balance of that year was read on the sixth of Longdark.

The hands roll the great map and slide it into a leather case.
The same hands slide **a smaller sheet, the same coastline,** under the blotter. (No comment. Two seconds. *Fixed.*)

The case is lifted and carried out of frame, up a stair.

MATCH CUT (the stair → a spiral of black stone, climbing).
