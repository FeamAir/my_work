
## SC 8 — INT. PL-0014 THE CROWN SPIRE, BASE AND INNER STAIR (inside PL-0009) — AFTERNOON [7:54–9:04]

Black stone, spiral-grooved, curving up out of sight. Light from above, cold and white.

IONE stands very straight at the foot of the stair while a hooded NOVICE-ATTENDANT (bk) fusses at her hem. Eighteen; tall, slender, upright. White, with a novice's linen stole; a violet band at the throat; pale silver hair to the waist, two thin side-plaits joined behind. A silver tuning fork hangs at her breastbone.

SOUND: almost nothing. Cloth. The stair's own faint ring when a foot touches it. Far below, the palace.

ATTENDANT [OS] (brisk; a servant who has dressed many of these)
Sing it flat for the lords, Lady Ione. They like to hear what they have paid for.

IONE [MS] (clear, light, precise; she means the question)
Is that what the song is for?

ATTENDANT [OS]
It is what today is for.

Ione does not answer. She lifts the fork from its cord, strikes it once on her knuckle (generated insert: hands) and holds its foot to the bone behind her ear. Her eyes close.
SOUND: the fork's pure tone, heard as she hears it, from inside the head. Everything else drops away.

She opens her eyes. The grooves of the stair wind upward past her.

On the third turn an OLD CANTOR (bk: robe, a yellowed throat-band) stands aside to let her pass. One hand rests at the throat. The other makes a short, tired sign in the air: a blessing. The mouth moves with it. **No sound comes out.**

Ione stops. She bows to the old Cantor, lower than she needs to, the way one bows to something one is going to become. Then she climbs.

SOUND: her steps on the spiral → wind → open air.

## SC 9 — EXT. PL-0014 THE CROWN OF THE CROWN SPIRE → PL-0018 HIGHWATER HARBOUR → a roof-ridge in PL-0008 — LATE AFTERNOON [9:04–10:54]

*The image of the series. Ione is never seen from the front while she sings.*

[9:04] An open throat of black stone at the top of the tower, sky all round it. Ione stands in it, **back to camera**, white robes snapping, hair streaming. Far below and in front of her: the harbour in a hard wind. Chop, swell, a hundred masts swinging out of time with each other; among them a flagship with a cormorant at her bow. (The composition is the tapestry's last panel.)

SOUND: wind, rigging, gulls, waves on the mole: loud, busy, everywhere. No music.

She breathes in.

[9:16] **One sustained tone.** A young, trained, true soprano, no ornament, no vibrato.
MUSIC: none. The voice is the only music.

[9:22] The stone answers. A hum arrives **under** her, felt in the feet before it is heard. The spiral grooves light blue-white from inside, turn by turn, **down** the whole tower, like water finding a thread.

[9:34] THE HARBOUR, one continuous shot, no cut between the two states: the chop shortens. The swell lies down. Then nothing. **The water is glass.** The masts stand still, all together. A gull crosses, and its reflection is exact.
SOUND: the sea is **taken away**: waves, rigging, gulls fade to nothing while she sings, until there is only the tone and the hum under it. The flattening is heard as subtraction.
SDH: `[one sung tone]` `[the tower hums]` `[the sea goes silent]`

Hold it. Four seconds of a city that has stopped moving.

[9:58] CROSS-CUT. A roof-ridge in the Ninth, low sun. Pell and Wick sit astride it. In Pell's lap the rag, open: four copper coins.

WICK [MS] (to the coins)
Four. The rag-man robbed us.

Pell is not looking at the coins. Far up the hill the black tower is burning white from crown to foot.
CLOSE on Pell, silent: for one unguarded second she is simply looking at the most beautiful thing she has ever seen. Then it goes out of her face.

Below their roof the water in the lane has only **slowed**. It still moves. A bucket turns in it.

WICK [OS]
It cares up there.

PELL [MS] (dry; to the tower, not to him) **FIXED**
Water doesn't care who you are.

Her hand, without her noticing, has found the cord at her neck. (The stone stays under the oilskin. It is not in frame.)

[10:36] THE CROWN. The tone ends. The hum goes on a breath longer and stops. The grooves fade from the top down.
Ione's hand goes to her throat. She swallows. Swallows again. Turns, at last a quarter-profile, no more, and starts down.
SOUND: the first wave comes back against the mole, small.

## SC 13 — INT. PL-0011 THE LANTRY COUNTING HOUSE, THE COUNTING HALL — LATE AFTERNOON, THE SAME HOUR `LA` [10:54–12:09]

*The quietest place in the episode. No music in any Counting House scene.*

Through tall windows: the Crown Spire, its last grooves still lit; the harbour lying flat.
Inside: felt floors. Forty desks in rows, forty identical ash-grey backs. Ledgers turned with ivory blades. On every desk a glass of water, and in every glass **the surface is dead flat.** Nobody looks up at the tower. One or two look at the glass.
As the light on the Spire dies, a tremble comes back into the water. The pens have not stopped.

SOUND: pens. A page. Felt underfoot. Nothing else.

HOLLIS, at a desk like the others and too tall for it: twenty-six, very thin, stooped. Straw hair with one cowlick at the crown that no comb has ever beaten. Round spectacles, the **left** lens cracked. Ink on two fingers of the right hand. A light ash-grey coat. He is casting a long column, the pen-point ticking down it.

HOLLIS [VO] (light, dry, quick; a young man thinking in figures; nothing of the Chronicler's pace)
Forty-one lines. No deed-district. No street. No name against any of it.
(the pen reaches the foot)
And it adds.

He looks at the total as if it had said something rude.

HOLLIS [VO] (CONT'D)
A column that adds on the first cast is either honest or very well made. Again.

The pen goes back to the top. Down. The same total.

INSERT: the head of the page, in a copperplate hand. No title, no district. Only: **ACCOUNT NINE**. Hold it a full two seconds.

HOLLIS [MS] (aloud; to nobody; under his breath)
Not yet reconciled.

His eye, out of habit, runs on down the page on the next desk.

## SC 10 — INT. PL-0009 THE DRY PALACE, THE TITHE HALL — LATE AFTERNOON, THE SUN LOW [12:09–14:14]

A long hall. A dais, a map-table, one tall window on the harbour, which lies like a mirror. Eight banners; under them **eight blocks of colour** seen from behind or from the gallery: white; deep blue; rust red; sea green; slate and sulphur; black and copper; violet and linen; and the Helm's own. Stewards' backs in some of the chairs. (Optional, cut-safe: among the violet, one small veiled figure, a bowed attendant at her shoulder. No line, no sign.) By the door, a man in slate with a small silver pin shaped like a hare's ear (bk).

SOUND: room tone, cloth, low talk. Almost no music. At most a warm held colour under the entrance.

THE HELM is already at the map-table: old, tall, stooped, frail, a square white beard, a plain sickle ring on the **right** hand. The leather map-case lies in front of him. When he shifts his weight, the whole hall shifts with him. He is the man they all turn to.

Then the doors, and the room turns the other way.

RUEL comes in last: fifty or so, big, heavy-chested, sea-weathered. A deep-blue long coat with a silver cormorant clasp; salt-and-pepper hair in a short sailor's queue; a grey-streaked square beard. A rolled petition in his **right** hand. Behind his shoulder, a young officer (bk only: a short deep-blue jacket, dark umber curls tied high with a silver cord). She does not turn round in this episode.

He stops for the door-warden.

RUEL [MS] (warm, round, broad; Gullmouth vowels; a voice people lean toward)
And that girl of yours? Still climbing my rigging when the watch isn't looking?

The door-warden's back straightens with pleasure. Faces along the benches are smiling before Ruel has reached them. He is the man you would follow into a boat.

At the map-table. Two men who have had this conversation before; they keep it low. Play it on hands, the map-case, the petition, and on the listener.

RUEL [MS] (quiet)
Pumps for the low sluices. It's all written down. You need only say yes.

THE HELM [OS] (old, rasping, tired)
You will have my answer when I have read.

RUEL [MS]
I know what you'll read.
(beat)
Read it differently.

THE HELM [MS]
It is drawn, Ruel. I only read it.

RUEL [OS]
People live down there, old friend.

THE HELM [MS] (not unkind)
And you will lose this one.

Ruel looks at him for a moment. Then the big smile comes back, for the hall as much as for the old man.

RUEL [MS] **FIXED**
We go under and come up.

The petition is in his right hand. He holds out his **left**, and takes the Helm's hand in it. A long, warm clasp, the old hand almost hidden in the big one.
INSERT (one, two seconds, no more): the clasp. On Ruel's left hand, **a black pearl ring**, against the old man's knuckle.

IONE arrives at the foot of the dais from the stair door, a little out of breath, the fork back at her breastbone. Her voice has lost its top.

IONE [MS] (hoarse; formal)
Helm. The Cantor's word: the water lies flat.

The Helm nods. As his hand comes away from Ruel's:
INSERT: **a bead of blood** on the old man's knuckle. He looks at it without interest and puts the knuckle to his mouth, like a man who has caught a splinter.
CLOSE on Ione, silent: she has seen it. A ring; a scratch. She lets it go.

RUEL [MS] (kindly; to her)
Ione Ilvane. You've flattened my harbour. My captains will be bored.

A small bow from her, a smaller smile. The hall settles into its seats.

## SC 15 — INT. PL-0011 THE COUNTING HALL — TOWARD EVENING [16:14–17:44]

At the tap, every back in the hall straightens.

DAME URSEL LANTRY comes down the aisle: sixty-odd, small, upright. Black and copper; iron-grey hair in a tight coil with copper pins; pince-nez on a copper chain; a stick with a scaled handle; a copper ring on the right hand. She does not hurry. She stops at Hollis's desk, **not** at the next one, and reads his column upside down, faster than he read it the right way up.

URSEL [MS] (soft, low, slow; a grandmother's voice)
Mr Kett. You have cast it twice.

HOLLIS [MS] (on his feet, stooping; slowly, the way he speaks when he is about to be brave)
It balances, Dame Lantry. I can make it balance. What it *is*
(he looks for the word, and finds his own)
is not yet reconciled.

At the next desk a blotter has stopped moving.

URSEL [MS] (gently)
Then you have done exactly what was asked, Hollis.

From her sleeve: a small brass ledger-clip. She lays it on the head of the page, square on the two words there.

URSEL [LIPSYNC-CU] (still; kind; the pince-nez catching the window) **FIXED**
A clerk who is trusted gets a clip.

She is already moving on down the aisle.

URSEL [OS] (without turning) **FIXED**
All water finds its level.

Hollis stands a moment longer than he should. Then he picks up the clip, as one picks up something warm, and slides it into his **left** breast pocket.

At the next desk the pleasant smile has gone. The sleeve-guards are very white.

SOUND: Ursel's stick, going away on the felt.

## SC 15a — EXT. PL-0008 THE LANE OUTSIDE NAN'S STILT-HOUSE — LOW SUN [17:44–18:19]

The tide is coming back. Under the stilts a tongue of grey water slides in over the morning's mud, fills a footprint, fills the next. The lane plate of Sc 2, wetter, gold-edged.

SOUND: water creeping; planks; **a knock**, four doors up. The wheel's count, far off.

Pell sits on the plank-walk with her feet over the edge. The rag is open on her knee. She counts the four coins from one hand into the other. It is still four.

Four doors up, the slate-and-sulphur tabard. A woman hands a cooking-pot out of a doorway without a word; the collector hangs it on his arm with the others and goes to the next door. Knock. Three doors up.

Pell looks the other way, along the plank-walks toward the dike road. Lanterns being lit. No small hunched figure with a bale.

WICK [OS] (from the door behind her; not a joke this time)
She said dusk.

PELL [MS]
So did he.
