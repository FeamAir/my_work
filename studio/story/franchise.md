Version 1.2 · 2026-11-22 · author: lead-screenwriter · status: **concept**

The prequels stay compatible, with three guard-rails: P3 keeps the Queen's last song at a distance; P5 keeps one point of view closed; P2 gains an object. Release gates are unchanged, except that P3 has a second gate for its uncut final episode.

The nine warden families are six Spire-wardens and three sword-families (DEC-01); the Corvane admiral of OS 700 is named and comes ashore at the end (DEC-14); the Breaking spears are a registered object (DEC-12).
Spoiler level: **internal** (references sealed secrets by ID; full answers in `sealed/master_mystery.md` §3).

## 0. Rules

1. **The main saga is the trunk.** A prequel may *show* what the saga only tells, but must never contradict it. Everything the saga says about the past is either **true** or a **listed official lie** (`sealed/master_mystery.md` §6.2). Prequels show the truth; their own in-world chronicles may repeat the lie of their time.
2. **Release gate:** a prequel is released only after the main-saga episode that reveals the secret it depends on (column "Release not before").
3. **Own title under the common brand** (`HOLLOWSTONE: The Many`, `HOLLOWSTONE: Crown of Keepers`, `HOLLOWSTONE: The Breaking`, `HOLLOWSTONE: The Stair`, `HOLLOWSTONE: Still Water` — r3), **own heroes, own mysteries** — each prequel is a complete story with its own layered mystery (episode/season level) and adds at least one new layer to a saga secret *without* changing its answer.
4. **Room in the calendar:** the era scale (`series_bible.md` §8) reserves BA 1–~400 (abs 0–399) for the Builders' Age; OS has 700 years; CY 1–311 is pre-saga. The keeper records prequel events as normal `EV-` records with `canon: draft` and a `franchise:` note in prose.
5. Objects, places and names that appear in both must match the canon (the spiral shell, the Lirenne tapestry, the Corvane cormorant, the Ilvane "Breaking spears" — white-bronze leaf spearheads stamped with the open hand, kept as relics at Pale Crag; r3).

## 1. Prequel slate

### P1 — *THE MANY* (Builders' Age, ~BA 1–60)
- **Logline:** On a reef where the sea takes a child every winter, a fisherwoman discovers that wet stone hums back when the whole village sings — and three generations must keep singing to a rock that will not finish growing in their lifetimes.
- **Heroes:** a reef-town chorus-leader; her son who hates the song; a rival town that wants the stone for itself.
- **Own mysteries:** why the stone answers some towns and not others; who poisoned the first well (episode/season level).
- **Links to the saga:** shows SEC-014 (the people grew the Spires) and SEC-021 (the full chord) at their origin; the origin of the spiral sign and of the Still Days as a people's festival; the five-note lullaby is first a work-chant here.
- **Release not before:** S8E10 (SEC-014 revealed). Ideal: between S10 and S11.
- **Look/sound:** no towers yet — reefs, stilts, a stone stump; acapella chorus music. Cheap to produce (small sets, crowds in silhouette).

### P2 — *CROWN OF KEEPERS* (Old Song, ~OS 380–420)
- **Logline:** The elected Keeper of Lirenne dies in a storm; his daughter Isembar decides that the song is too important to leave to elections — and the nine families she raises to guard the Spires will one day destroy her line.
- **Heroes:** Isembar Ashare; the first Ilvane warden (a choir teacher who invents solo singing); a Corvane captain; a Lows chorus-mother who opposes the crown.
- **Own mysteries:** who sabotaged the Storm Spire that killed the old Keeper; the first "gleaned" child.
- **Links:** origin of the nine warden families — six that are given Spires to conduct and three sword-families sworn without one (r3; fans' house maps go deeper), of the hereditary Keepership, of the Ilvane schools ("the solo heresy" — seed of SEC-005); the Builders stay a half-remembered legend ("the First Voices"), so SEC-014 is not spoiled. *r2:* the Keeper's ring (a hollow black pearl holding the pitch-stone) is made for Isembar on screen — as regalia only; its later fate is the saga's (SEC-027).
- **Release not before:** S5E10 (SEC-003/005 revealed). Must keep the Builders' origin as legend.
- **Look/sound:** Lirenne at its height — teal and pearl, the Sunken Spire alive; court music + people's chorus.

### P3 — *THE BREAKING* (Old Song, OS 690–700)
- **Logline:** In the last ten years of the Tide-Kings, Queen Neveh plans to give the Spires back to the people — and her servant girl Oshra is the only one who sees the conspiracy of the Nine closing around the Still Days.
- **Heroes:** Oshra (servant, POV), Queen Neveh, Matriarch Sereth Ilvane (antagonist with her own logic), a Corvane admiral torn between oath and family (Tolvar Corvane: he holds the harbour with his ships and comes ashore only when the city falls — r3).
- **Own mysteries:** which of the nine wardens tried to warn the Queen; the lost tenth verse.
- **Links:** shows SEC-003 in full; Pell's ancestry (Oshra); Maren's ancestor's role; the Lirenne tapestry is woven in-story by a Nine-paid weaver (the lie being made); the founding of the Quiet; the Hush Law's real purpose.
- **Release not before:** S5E10. Strongest prequel — candidate for the first spin-off (between S6 and S7).
- **Guard-rail (r2):** if released before S10E10, the climax is told strictly from Oshra's reed boat: she hears the Queen's voice from the crown, then the sea. What the Queen sang, and why the city sank, is not explained — the series must stay readable both as "the chord broke" (what the saga's audience believes until S10) and as the truth (SEC-025). The "lost tenth verse" stays a mystery of P3, as planned. A full final episode from the Queen's side may be released only after S10E10. The Corvane admiral takes a ring from the Queen's hand in a wide shot, uncommented.
- **Look/sound:** the drowning of a city as the climax (AI-friendly: water, crowds, night).

### P4 — *THE STAIR* (Concord Years, CY 136–141)
- **Logline:** The lords of Vanth discover that their Spire is starving the uplands — and choose to sing it less, knowing the Concord will call it treason.
- **Heroes:** Lady of Vanth; a Rusk captain sent to "restore order"; an Ossari kite-rider envoy; a Choir inspector.
- **Own mysteries:** who betrayed Vanth's secret trade to the Choir; what Vanth wrote in the "river book" that Rusk burned.
- **Links:** SEC-008 (the thirst) seen 180 years earlier; Kadu's ancestry; how Rusk won the Stair (house history); the First Upland War as a big-battle series.
- **Release not before:** S7E10 (SEC-008 revealed).
- **Look/sound:** escarpment, falls, kites, early pike and shot.

### P5 (short, 6 episodes) — *STILL WATER* (CY 295–296)
- **Logline:** One night in the Lows: a mother, a young soldier at a gate, a sea captain in a rowboat, a clerk with a pen, and an old woman in Choir robes who takes a singing baby.
- **Heroes:** Liss, Kes (17), Ruel (31), Teodor Kett, Sister Venn.
- **Links:** SEC-004, SEC-006, SEC-009, SEC-015 precedent ("Account Five").
- **Release not before:** S6E8 (last of those reveals). Ideal as an anthology between S6 and S7.
- **Guard-rails (r2):** the First Cantor appears only veiled and unvoiced, with no point of view — what she recognises in the baby's lullaby is the saga's secret (SEC-026); the Pumpwrights' "breakage" that week may be shown (SEC-028 is revealed in S5E8, before the gate); Liss already wears a small dark stone on a cord (no comment).

## 2. Sequel space

The saga ends with a new count of years (the "Year of Many Voices") and an epilogue decades later. A sequel era is possible (new problems of a world where everyone can sing), but **no sequel is planned**; the epilogue must not promise one.
