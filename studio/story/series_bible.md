## 1. Title

| | Title | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Working title** | **THE LOW NOTE** | Triple meaning: a musical note missing from the world's magic; the "low" people of the flooded districts; a low moment of a civilisation. Short, searchable, musical — fits a saga whose mystery is literally hidden in the soundtrack. |
| Alternative 1 | **HOLLOWSTONE** | The black singing stone of the Spires; strong brand, good for merch and the logo. |
| Alternative 2 | **NINE SPIRES** | Clean "epic map" title; tells the viewer at once that there are nine seats of power. |

Season subtitles follow the pattern `THE LOW NOTE — Book One: Dry Tithe`.

## 2. Logline

> When the singing towers that keep a sinking delta empire dry begin to fall silent, a mudlark girl from the drowning slums, a novice of the singing nobility, a bank clerk who finds that the empire's money is a lie, a border sergeant and the heirs of rival lords are dragged into elections, floods and invasion — and into a three-hundred-year-old lie about who the song really belongs to.

Short version (for thumbnails/Shorts): *An empire stays dry only while its nobles sing. The poor are forbidden to sing. Now the song is failing.*

## 3. Genre and tone

- **Genre:** political epic fantasy + long-form mystery ("low fantasy with one law of wonder"). No dragons, no prophecies, no chosen one, no levels.
- **Tone:** *Shōgun*-level stakes and politics, *Apothecary Diaries*-style puzzle per episode, *Frieren*-style moments of quiet and memory. Grounded, wet, lamp-lit. Humour comes from people (Hollis's dry wit, Wick's mouth, the Lows' street culture), never from parody.
- **Rating:** TV 16+. Battle violence shown through outcome, sound and silhouette; no torture porn; no sexual content; **no romance involving characters under 18** (Pell 15, Wick 11, Esk 13, Zev 14, Daro 16 at the start).
- **Core feeling:** water rising slowly; everyone knows the bill will come due — the question is who pays.

## 4. Setting — and why it is fresh

**The Concord of Tessary** is a great river delta and barrier archipelago at the mouth of the river **Aun**, most of it *below sea level*. It stays dry only because of nine colossal towers of black singing stone — the **Spires** — built by a forgotten people **(belief: "the Builders", a vanished race)**. When a trained **Cantor** sings into a Spire, the stone answers and the sea obeys: storms flatten, currents turn, water stands back from the dikes.

Whoever holds a Spire decides which districts stay dry. Seven Spires are held by great powers; one is dying; one has been under the sea for 312 years. The ruler — **the Helm** — is *elected for life* by a Conclave of eight votes. Money is printed by a bank against deeds to dry land. The poor live below the waterline in **the Lows**, pay a **dry-tithe** for protection, and are **forbidden by law to sing**.

The Spires have been **slowly detuning** for three centuries. Engineers with pumps and gunpowder are rising. Upland clans whose rivers are drying are looking down at the green delta.

**Why it is fresh (vs. the market, `research/market.md` §2):**
1. Power = **water, money and information**, not a throne. Intrigue is natural at every level: who floods, who profits, who knows first.
2. Magic is **music** — a rule nobody else in the genre owns. The mystery can be hidden in the score; fans can *hear* clues. It directly serves the investor's wish for music that "hooks".
3. Magic has a **visible price** (the singer's voice) — and that price is an AI-production gift: many key characters speak little or not at all.
4. Original culture synthesis: canals, terraces, lantern-lit stilt towns, treadmill pumps, kites over plateaus — no single real culture copied, not pseudo-England.
5. A ticking clock that is **social, not monstrous**: the sea is coming, and the rich have decided whose streets go first.

**How it looks on screen:** Spires are black, glassy, spiral-grooved towers (60–200 m), crowned by a ring of open "throats". A singing Cantor stands in a throat at the crown, back to camera, robes snapping — **an iconic, repeatable, lip-sync-free shot**.

## 7. Society, classes, power and money

### 7.1 The three tiers
| Tier | Who | How they live | Their game (schemes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Lows** (poor, ~60%) | mudlarks, sluice-workers, salt-rakers, treadmill walkers, boat-people | Below sea level, pay the **dry-tithe**, flood every few years, forbidden to sing | Smuggling echo-shards; the **Undertow** syndicate sells "dry-pledges" (sandbag protection racket); rumour networks; secret singing; salvage rights fights; fake flood-drowning to escape debts |
| **The Mids** (middle, ~30%) | clerks, pumpwrights, relay-keepers, soldiers, shopkeepers, low clergy | On the dike level — dry unless a big breach | Pumpwrights' Guild sabotages old pumps to sell new ones; clerks forge, skim and sell ledger secrets; relay-keepers sell messages early; soldiers sell gate passes during floods |
| **The Steps** (rich and rulers, ~10%) | great houses, bankers, Cantors, Conclave | On the granite hill and crags; never flood | Vote-buying; grain cornering; **flood speculation** (buying land the bank knows will be "let go"); falsified relay messages; the Choir's sale of "priority songs" to whoever pays most |

### 7.2 How power works
- **Spires = votes = water.** A great house holds a Spire, therefore a vote, therefore a say over the **Tithe Map** — the yearly plan of which districts get sung first in a storm.
- **The Helm** (elected for life) commands the Concord fleet, holds the Crown Spire in trust and signs the Tithe Map. Addressed as "Helm" or "Your Steadiness". Seat: the **Dry Palace** in Highwater.
- **The Conclave (the Ring)** — 8 votes: Corvane, Dask, Ilvane, Rusk, Quell, Fennick, **the Choir** (as a body), **Lantry** (the bank bought the extinct House Harrow's seat in CY 252 — "the Purchased Seat"). Ninth, vacant: **the Sunken Vote**.
- **Law:** House Fennick runs the Hearings (courts) and the Relay. Justice is bought, delayed or fast-tracked. Commoners cannot testify against a noble without a Mids sponsor.
- **Army and fleet:** each great house keeps its own troops; the Concord fleet is mostly Corvane ships under the Helm's flag (a permanent tension). Mercenary "free companies" are hired with dry-notes.

### 7.3 Money
- **Marks** (silver coin) — real but scarce.
- **Dry-notes** — paper issued by the Lantry Counting House, each "a promise of dry ground": backed by deeds to land above the waterline. Everyone uses them. When land floods, the notes backed by it are worthless → **whoever knows in advance which districts will be let flood can get rich** — the central financial scheme of Act I–II.
- **The dry-tithe** — protection tax paid by every household in the Lows to the house holding the nearest Spire.
- **Grain** — Dask controls most of it; hoarding before a flood season is a political weapon.
- **Saltpeter and echo-stones** — strategic goods; the uplands hold the first, the Relay the second.

### 7.4 Religion
- **The Choir** — official faith and order of Cantors: venerates the **First Voices** (the Builders) and the Spires as their gift. Cantors are priests; the **First Cantor** heads the order. Novices are trained from childhood; children found with the ear are "gleaned" into the Choir.
- **The Lady Below** — Lows folk faith: the sea as a drowned mother who takes back what she is owed. Offerings of salt and shells; the **spiral shell** is her sign. Tolerated, mocked by the Steps.

## 9. Through-line themes

1. **Who pays for dry land?** Every scheme, war and miracle asks this — from the treadmill walker to the uplands.
2. **Voice:** who is allowed to speak, sing, testify, vote. The cost of the song is literally one's voice.
3. **History is written by the dry.** The in-world **Chronicle** that narrates the series is partly false; viewers learn to doubt the narrator.
4. **Debt** — money, blood, guilt. Every inheritance in this saga is also a bill.
5. **The many and the one:** solo heroism vs. collective action. The saga's final answer is musical.

## 10. Storytelling devices (fixed for all seasons)

- **The Chronicler** — an old, dry voice reading from *The Chronicle of the Dry Years* over maps, tapestries and documents in cold opens and season intros. His version is the official one. Treat every Chronicler line as *possibly wrong*. (Voice character: old, precise, faintly hoarse.)
- **Episode puzzle** — most episodes contain one small solvable puzzle (a forged note, a stolen tithe chest, a false relay tone) closed inside the episode.
- **Mystery leitmotif** — Nan's lullaby (a five-note phrase). It appears ONLY in clue scenes. Composer: see `studio/music/` once created.
- **House leitmotifs** — each Spire has a "home tone"; each house theme is built on its Spire's tone.
- **The spiral** — visual anchor of the main mystery (shell, Spire grooves, chalk sign).
- **Delivery tags in scripts:** `[VO]`, `[OS]`, `[MS]`, `[LIPSYNC-CU]` (per `research/recommendations.md` §B). Characters who lost their voice speak in **hand-sign + subtitles or an interpreter's VO** — a deliberate production advantage.
- **Battles** are written as: council over the map → wide shots of formations/fleets/kites → 2–3 personal threads → the prepared twist → aftermath and the new map.

## 12. Continuity rules (priority #1)

The saga must feel like one woven cloth. Every writer, director and editor follows these rules.

### 12.1 Episode → episode
1. **Hand-off scene.** Every episode opens by *picking up the last image or line* of the previous one — the same place, the same moment, or a direct consequence of it (no "meanwhile, three weeks later" without a Chronicler bridge).
2. **One thread carried, one thread passed.** Each episode resolves at least one beat from the previous episode and ends by handing a concrete question to the next.
3. **The cold open is caused, not decorative.** It either shows a consequence of last week's cliffhanger, or a Chronicler document that will matter in this episode.
4. **Cliffhangers must be paid in the next episode** (even if the payoff opens a bigger question). No cliffhanger is dropped.
5. **Line limit:** 2–3 storylines per episode; a POV absent for 3 episodes gets a one-line Chronicler or letter bridge so the viewer never loses them.
6. **Travel and time are shown or stated** — a character never "teleports"; the Chronicler or an on-screen map stamp gives date and place when a line jumps.

### 12.2 Season → season
1. **Every season finale ends on an image that the next season's E1 cold open answers** (the same place/object seen from another side).
2. **Season-opening "map" sequence:** E1 of every season begins with the Chronicler over the updated map of the Spires and houses (who holds what now). This is also the fan "board update".
3. **Each season closes at least one old mystery and opens new ones** (S1–S6: close 1, open 2; S7–S12: close 2, open 1). The closed mystery must have been opened in an earlier season.
4. **Time skips (only one: S4→S5, five years)** are bridged by the Chronicler and by a montage of objects that changed hands.
5. **A dead character keeps acting** through letters, debts, children, oaths, objects (`research/what_hooks.md` §2). Each major death names in the outline *what of theirs keeps moving the plot*.

### 12.4 How clues return across seasons
- Every clue is logged in `sealed/master_mystery.md` with: episode planted → episode(s) it is *echoed* → reveal episode. **A clue is echoed at least once before its reveal** (a second angle, a line that reframes it, or the leitmotif on it).
- After every reveal, the reveal episode **shows a 3–6-shot "recall" montage of the earlier clues** (fair-play proof; reuses existing shots → cheap for AI production).
- The **Chronicler's errors** are consistent: he always errs in favour of the dry (the Steps). When he is wrong, he is wrong the same way each time — so attentive fans can map his bias.
- No reveal uses a rule, object or person not established at least one season earlier.

### 12.5 Dialogue continuity
- Each main character has a **speech fingerprint** (catch-phrase, register, what they never say) in their dossier; the keeper checks it.
- Names, titles and forms of address follow `canon/glossary.md`; a title change (Lady → Helm) changes how everyone addresses them from that episode on.
- Lines that will gain a second meaning later are tagged in scripts `[ECHO: SEC-xxx]` so the editor never cuts them.
